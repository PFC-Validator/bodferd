Private Fortified Communications.

Private reports from THORChain node operators to maintainers.

In the proposed first version, you submit through Tor; maintainers receive an encrypted email attachment. Your computer signs and encrypts the report before sending it. A relay checks your authorization and forwards the encrypted package without being able to read its contents.

How a report is delivered

  1. You register your identity. The node operator authorizes your own communication key. Several team members can be authorized for the same node.
  2. You prepare a report on your computer. The Bodferd command-line tool signs your message and optional file, then encrypts them for the approved maintainers.
  3. You send it through Tor to a relay. The relay checks your authorization and forwards the encrypted package to the designated maintainer mailbox. It cannot read the contents.
  4. A maintainer decrypts and verifies it locally. Their tool checks who sent it and whether anything changed before presenting the report. If email fails, they can retrieve the package directly through Tor while it remains retained.
  5. You check for acknowledgement through Tor. A signed maintainer acknowledgement confirms receipt; an email delivery status alone does not. The design also allows an encrypted reply through the same private status channel.

Discord carries public registration announcements. The blockchain helps verify node authority and the current approved recipient policy. Neither carries your confidential report.

The first version is a reporting channel. Private conversations and group chat are a later direction.

Two channels, one chain of trust

Public enrollment establishes who may sign. Confidential transport delivers reports without giving the relay a decryption key. These channels have different privacy boundaries.

Tor conceals connection location; the node signature identifies the sender to the admission relay. Static age recipient keys do not provide forward secrecy. These are explicit limits, not implementation defects to hide.

Register two keys, not one

An age recipient is for encryption. Future message signatures use a separate Ed25519 communication key. The registration binds both keys to the node, service, environment, expiry and permitted actions.

  1. Create keys locally. Keep the communication signing key and reply age identity on the operator’s machine.
  2. Prepare a scoped registration. Bind both keys, node authority, key epoch and certificate expiry in a stable registration core; receive a separate one-use challenge.
  3. Announce its core digest with make relay. Only the public registration digest goes to Discord.
  4. Verify authority and possession. Check the authorizer against independent chain state and prove control of both newly registered keys.

When only the unmodified Discord announcement is available, designated registrars verify it and issue policy-authorized attestations. Production requires two separately administered witnesses and explicitly accepts trust in the existing relay/bot. Screenshots and usernames are insufficient.

No compatible sign-only adapter or raw-envelope exporter has yet been demonstrated. Until then, enrollment requires explicitly authorized witnesses; direct cryptographic enrollment is a future integration gate.

Unenrolled operators use make relay for a public, non-sensitive contact request. Channel monitoring prompts follow-up; confidential reports require enrollment.

Maintainer keys need an independent role-authorizing policy. A node signature can establish node authority; it cannot appoint its signer as a THORChain maintainer.

One node, several responsible people

A node operator can authorize several team members. Each person uses their own communication signing key and reply age identity; routine reporting needs no shared node secret.

  • Independent grants. The node/operator authority approves each member’s keys, permissions and expiry. Adding a teammate does not replace anyone else.
  • Optional personal identity. Send using the node and a pseudonymous credential, or add a name/handle inside the signed encrypted report. Names are self-described; the verified fact is node-authorized key possession.
  • Separate offboarding. Rotate or revoke one member without disabling others. A node-wide emergency freeze still affects everyone.
  • Shared limits. Team members share the operator’s quotas and incident capacity. Extra accounts do not create extra allowance.
  • Private replies. A response returns to the submitting member. Team-wide history sharing needs separate, explicit authorization.

Omitting a name does not hide the credential: maintainers and the admission relay can still distinguish keys and correlate their use. Future group messaging gives each person/device its own membership.

From local file to verified report

  1. Verify the recipient policy. Verify dual-authorized reader keys and fresh on-chain policy currentness; reject substitution, revocation or rollback before encrypting.
  2. Package and sign the content. Include the message and optional file/filename and an inner signed manifest. Encrypt the package locally with age.
  3. Sign admission context. Bind the ciphertext hash and size to the service, recipient policy, environment, registration, challenge and submission ID.
  4. Upload over Tor. The relay verifies authorization and quotas before accepting a bounded body. Atomic durable state prevents replay from creating duplicate accepted jobs.
  5. Deliver encrypted email with Tor recovery. Original evidence and ciphertext are wrapped in another age envelope so the mail provider cannot read the node signature. Retain a Tor-retrievable copy until maintainer acknowledgement or the bounded deadline; SMTP acceptance alone does not delete it.
  6. Verify at the maintainer. Decrypt into staging, check both signatures and all file hashes, then present the report. Never automatically open or execute attachments.
Admission is not decryption verification. An opaque relay can validate authorization, exact bytes and bounded age structure. Only the maintainer can establish that the report decrypts successfully.

Receipt states distinguish acceptance, queueing, SMTP acceptance, failure and a maintainer-signed acknowledgement. The operator retrieves acknowledgements and encrypted replies through a private Tor polling capability.

Security controls built into the design

The nineteen original findings (R00–R18) are mapped to ten control groups in the full reference. Thirty findings from the supplied external review have individual dispositions. These are proposed acceptance requirements; implementation tests have not been run.

  • Honest encryption claims: the maintainer verifies decryptability; the relay never claims to have decrypted.
  • Independent recipient trust: maintainer-side plus independent approval, confirmed bootstrap and independently verified on-chain currentness.
  • Scoped delegation: separate communication keys, short validity, fresh authority checks and durable revocation.
  • Exact signature binding: inner content authorship plus outer ciphertext and destination context.
  • Durable replay protection: one-use challenges, atomic outbox insertion and idempotent retries.
  • Defined operator eligibility: exact node/operator authority; unrelated bond providers excluded.
  • Tor-only submission: pinned onion, local SOCKS, no redirect or direct fallback.
  • Metadata minimization: encrypted filenames and sender proofs, generic email, bounded retention.
  • Abuse and file defenses: admission before upload capacity, parent quotas, global caps and staged verification.
  • Delivery and environment separation: fixed routes, disjoint authorities/keys, startup refusal and signed end-to-end acknowledgement.

A valid node signature does not establish that the report is true, the operator is uncompromised, or the attachment is safe. The relay can still suppress delivery. A compromised plaintext endpoint can still expose a report.

From reports to trusted conversations

Bodferd can grow into secure communication between node operators, THORNode developers and trusted groups. Its distinct job is to verify identities and authorize membership. Conversation encryption and device sessions should come from an established messaging system.

Stock Signal does not meet the Tor-only and automatic membership requirements. The owner selected MLS to lead the investigation, keeping a new client reusing libsignal as an alternative; neither supplies a complete Tor messenger or an established full audit for this integration. Static age recipients remain appropriate for the first reporting workflow.

  • Node authority, developer roles and group administration have separate sources of trust.
  • Every device needs explicit authorization; membership changes update conversation keys.
  • Removed members retain what they previously received. Future-message access ends after the removal takes effect.
  • All messaging traffic needs demonstrated Tor support, including background key fetches and notifications.

V1 provides reports and bounded encrypted replies. Group chat is a future direction with its own protocol, verification requirements and explicit permissions. The full design explains the costs and privacy boundaries of MLS and libsignal reuse, with scoped identity credentials and separately authorized groups.

What must be settled before implementation

The draft records the boundaries and owner decisions. Unproven publication, trust and operational prerequisites block implementation readiness and production use.

  • Confirm the maintainer reader roster, separately administered policy/registrar signers, release root and fingerprint channels.
  • Test node/wallet sign-only compatibility and access to original signed relay envelopes.
  • Prove the on-chain policy publication carrier and complete policy scan; establish independent fresh and archival chain verification.
  • Provision dedicated onion identities and separate test/production configuration.
  • Verify mail-provider TLS and attachment limits, then validate pilot resource and retention defaults.
  • Produce interoperable protocol fixtures and obtain independent cryptographic/parser review.

The first deployment uses synthetic data and the test recipient. Production starts only after the required controls have verification evidence.

A standalone project

PFC-Validator/bodferd is the standalone repository. It owns its trust policy, key lifecycle, storage and deployment. The relay has dedicated credentials and an onion-only admission boundary.
